Turning explicit map prediction into uncertainty exploration

Predicting the unobserved portion of a floor plan is informative only insofar as the prediction is not mistaken for observation. This work retains the predictive model but reassigns its role: regions of high predictive uncertainty become the targets of exploration.

Venue — IJCAS 2026 Topic — Object-goal navigation Code — [add link]

The problem

Modular object-goal navigation agents extend their map with a learned prediction of the space beyond the observed frontier. When such a prediction is treated as ground truth, an incorrect inference directs the agent toward geometry that does not exist, and the episode is typically lost.

The approach

The map predictor produces a distribution rather than a single map. Disagreement among samples defines an uncertainty field over unexplored space, and frontier selection is scored by expected information gain with respect to the target object rather than by predicted free space alone.

Implications

The agent no longer commits to unsupported geometry and instead allocates its step budget where uncertainty is greatest, yielding shorter trajectories in familiar layouts and more graceful degradation in unfamiliar ones where prediction is least reliable.
